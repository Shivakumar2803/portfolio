Featured project

Rainfall Intelligence & Predictive Analytics

A rainfall analytics dashboard for India that I built for Codesis. It covers 115 years of rainfall data (1901–2015) across 36 subdivisions and 641 districts.

Key figures shown in the dashboard

1,411 mmNational annual average
76.5%Share of SW monsoon (Jun–Sep)
+21.7%Peak anomaly year (1961)
4,757Records indexed

The problem

Rainfall data for India is spread over thousands of rows. It is hard to read by hand and hard to compare across states and years. Codesis needed one place to see trends, find extreme years and look up any record quickly.

What the dashboard does

Overview
National average, wettest and driest subdivision, monsoon share, a 115-year trend with a trendline, and top-10 subdivision rankings.
Rainfall Analytics
Pick a subdivision and year to see monthly and seasonal rainfall, plus the wettest and driest districts.
OLS Prediction
An OLS (linear regression) module for rainfall trend and prediction.
Anomaly & Risk
Labels each year as surplus (above +10%), normal or deficit (below -10%) and flags flood and drought years.
Data Explorer
Search and filter all records by state, district, subdivision or year.
Light, Night and Dark themes
Users can switch the look to suit their screen.

How I built it

Understand
Agreed the modules and the questions the dashboard must answer.
Prepare data
Used the IMD 115-year archive and cleaned and organised the records.
Analyse
Worked out averages, seasonal shares, anomalies and the OLS trend.
Present
Built five clear modules with charts, rankings and a searchable table.

What I learned

  • Clear requirements at the start save time later.
  • Clean data matters more than a complicated model.
  • A simple layout helps non-technical users trust the numbers.

Sample Business Requirements Document

A BRD for the rainfall dashboard. It shows how I write down a business problem so a team can build it and a client can approve it.

Business Requirements DocumentRainfall Intelligence & Predictive Analytics
ClientCodesis
Prepared byGuntuku Shiva Kumar
Version1.0 (sample)

1. Purpose

Rainfall in India changes from state to state and year to year. The client needs one dashboard to study past rainfall, spot extreme years and see a simple trend prediction. This document lists what the dashboard must do.

2. Business objectives

  • Replace manual reading of spreadsheets with quick, visual answers.
  • Help users find wet, dry, flood and drought years.
  • Let users compare subdivisions and districts without technical knowledge.

3. Scope

In scope
  • IMD data, 1901–2015, 36 subdivisions, 641 districts
  • Overview, Analytics, OLS Prediction, Anomaly and Risk, Data Explorer
  • Search and filters
  • Light, Night and Dark themes
Out of scope
  • Live weather feeds
  • Daily or hourly forecasts
  • Mobile app
  • SMS or email alerts

4. Stakeholders

RoleInterest
Codesis team (client)Approves requirements and receives the dashboard
Planners and analysts (end users)Study rainfall trends and risk
Developer and analystPrepares data and builds the dashboard

5. Business requirements

IDRequirementPriority
BR-01Show national rainfall summary: average, wettest and driest subdivision, monsoon share.High
BR-02Show a 115-year rainfall trend with a trendline and subdivision rankings.High
BR-03Let the user pick a subdivision and year to see monthly and seasonal rainfall.High
BR-04Provide an OLS-based prediction module.High
BR-05Classify each year as surplus (> +10%), normal or deficit (< -10%) and flag flood and drought risk.High
BR-06Offer a searchable data table with filters for all records.Medium
BR-07Offer Light, Night and Dark themes.Medium

6. Non-functional requirements

  • Pages and filters respond instantly, without a page reload.
  • Labels and charts are easy for non-technical users to read.
  • Runs in a standard web browser on a laptop.

7. Assumptions and risks

TypeDetailHandling
AssumptionThe dataset given is correct and complete enough to use.Confirm with the client early.
RiskMissing or odd values in some years or regions.Clean the data and note every change.
RiskYearly data limits how fine a prediction can be.State clearly that results are yearly.

8. Success criteria

  • The client can explore any subdivision, year or record from one dashboard.
  • Every requirement above is met and signed off.
